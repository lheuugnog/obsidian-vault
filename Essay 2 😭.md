### 1. [[Statistical and causal models of explanation]]
Week 4 Lec 1, Reading 2 (unsat triad)
**Deductive-Nomological (DN) Model**
* A deductive inference grounded in natural laws
* General laws + initial conditions -> explained phenomenon
* Newton's laws and initial conditions -> explain the planet's orbit

**Statistical Relevance (SR) Model**
* Explanation based on statistical relevance or conditional dependence
* A factor C is relevant to B (within A) if P(B|A, C) != P(B|A)
* Statistically relevant factors contribute to explanation
* Smoking increases the probability of lung cancer

**Causal Mechanical (CM) Model**
* Explanation grounded in physical causal processes and interactions
* Causal processes + interactions -> explained phenomenon
* Two billard balls collide -> their motion changes through casual interaction

**Model aim: Explanans -> Explanandum**

**Thinking**

2. How do they relate to explainability and interpretability in AI systems?
3. What if we replaced the casual model with a statistical one?

### 5. [[Virtue ethics and metaethical moral subjectivism]]

(wk 1 reading 2 virtue ethics, wk 8 reading 2 moral subjectivism, wk 2 reading 1 acm)

This part is strongly recommended to be watched in the first lecture of Week 8, delivered by Seb

Normative ethics: what we ought to do/who we ought to be
Applied ethics: how we apply those theories in practice

Meta-Ethics on the other hand, is about introspecting the nature of
ethics itself. It asks questions like
- what ethics is,
- how we come to know ethics,
- the semantics of moral judgements,
- whether there are moral facts,
- the psychology of morality,
- and so on.

In this course, we only look at one of these questions, and this question is
“Are moral judgements truth-apt?”

Truth-Apt: We call the sentences capable of being true or false truth-apt, the ones not capable of being true or false not true-apt
* "Grass is green" vs "Stop that thief!"
We call truth-apt sentences propositions

Moral Judgements are just sentences that express moral claims
* "Murder is wrong" or "Donating to charity is good"

Are moral judgements truth-apt?
* Moral Cognitivism
* Moral Non-Cognitivism

**Cognitivism**
* **Divine Command Theory:** Objective because God
* **Objective Moral Realism:** Objective without God; moral facts are part of the natural world.
* **Cultural Relativism:** Can be true or false but depends on the culture.
* **Moral Subjectivism:** Can be true or false but depends on the mind.
* **Error Theory:** Always false
	* Fictionalism: Trust morality like fiction and follow it
	* Nihilism: Don't treat moral sentences like fiction

**Non-Cognitivism**
* **Expressivism / Emotivism:** Moral judgements are just expressions (or particularly emotional expressions)
* **Prescriptivism:** Moral judgements are like instructions

**Fictionalism vs Nihilism**
* **Fictionalism:** Keep using "right/wrong" and "ought/ought not" - treat morality as a useful fiction for mobilization, education and coordination.
* **Nihilism:** Drop moral vocabulary; make decisions and communicate in terms of rules, preferences, cost-benefit, risk and contracts

**Scenario Comparison**
1. Plagiarism
	* **Fictionalism:** "Plagiarism is dishonest/immoral; it should be corrected and apologised for"
	* **Nihilism:** "Plagiarism violates policy X and triggers sanction Y; to reduce future risk we'll add training and checks"
2. AI Ethics in a Product
	* **Fictionalism:** "We should avoid bias and must not harm users"
	* **Nihilism:** "To limit legal and repuational risk, we'll implement bias monitoring and appeals; violations bring contractual penalties"

**Virtue Ethics vs Meta-ethical Moral Subjectivism**
Virtue ethics: Actions are right if they express or cultivate the virtues of a good person and fit practical wisdom (the objectivity of the human good?)
Subjectivism: A moral claim is true/false relative to a subject's (often the individual's) attitudes or approvals

**Thinking**
1. What are the differences between virtue ethics and moral subjectivism?
2. To what extent are virtue ethics and moral subjectivism compatible? Where do their underlying assumptions conflict, and how might constructive dialogue between them be established?

### 6. [[Moral gambits and moral responsibility]]

(week 8 lecture 2 4 conditions, week 9 reading 1 responsibility gap, moral gambit, LAWS)

Readings Week 8 Lecture 2 - *Digital Systems and Moral Responsibility*
* webcms3.cse.unsw.edu.au/COMP4920/25T3/resources/115362

* **Responsibility (obligation-sense):** an obligation to do something (eg. X has moral responsibility to assist Y)
* **Moral responsibility (accountability):** whether an agent bears the right relation to their actions such that they are apt for moral praise or moral blame.

**Relation to causal responsibility**
One may be causally responsible for an outcome they brought about, but this does not by itself entail moral responsibility for it

More specifically, moral responsibility occurs when some conditions hold. Four independently necessary and jointly sufficient conditions on being morally responsible for an action.
* Intentionality condition: the intentions of the agent to achieve a given effect.
* Causality condition: there has to be a causal connection between the decision/action of the agent and their effects.
* Consequence condition: the agent has to have an understanding of the effects of the decision/action, as well as of their moral value and the consequent blame/praise.
* Choice condition: the agent has some degree of freedom that allows him/her to choose among different patterns of actions.

The existence of a responsibility gap
* The first three conditions are problematic to satisfy when considering AI systems.
* Problems with meeting these conditions underpin the responsibility gap

Meaningful moral responsibility holds only when all four conditions are met and the relevant agents accept the ensuing blame or praise.
* Justified and fair moral responsibility is only attributed when all the four conditions specified in sect 2 are met. Also the agents have to be able to accept this moral responsibility as an element of their actions and decisions, and have to be able to take the blame/praise that follows as an assessment of their moral character. Meaningful moral responsibility is ascribed meeting all these three criteria.

The existence of a responsibility gap.
