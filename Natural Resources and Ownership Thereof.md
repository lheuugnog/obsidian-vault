Mbembe argues that the colonial logic of **ownership through extraction** has not disappeared; it has simply migrated from the physical world to the digital realm. 

**Colonial View**
* Historically, European colonial powers treated the Earth as a vast resource to be seized, divided, and commodified
* Mbembe describes this as a "planetary project" involving the "re-allocation of the earth's resources and their privatisation by those who had the greatest military might" (Readings, Week 9)
* This worldview relied on the **legal fiction** of **Terra Nullius** — the claim that land not being “improved” or exploited was effectively empty and thus available for occupation. (Slides, Week 9) 
* During projects such as the partition of Africa, imperial powers assumed that the Earth in its entirety belonged to them, legitimising large-scale reallocation and privatisation of natural resources. 
* Ownership was equated with military might, and the planet became an object of extraction rather than a shared habitat.

**Post-Colonial, Normative View**
* Mbembe’s normative stance rejects this logic. 
* He argues that **“the earth belongs to no one”**, emphasising instead our interdependence and shared vulnerability. 
* For Mbembe, the Earth must be understood not as property but as **“our shared roof and our shared shelter.”** (Readings, Week 9)
* Shift from **ownership to stewardship** requires that we move beyond extractivist thinking and recognise planetary resources as a collective inheritance. 
* Stewardship foregrounds responsibility, care, and long-term sustainability over profit and exclusive control.

**Digital Colonialism**
* Crucially, Mbembe extends this critique into the digital age. 
* The colonial mindset that once justified the enclosure of land is replicated today in the enclosure of **data**. The rise of AI and digital infrastructure has produced what scholars call **Digital Colonialism**, in which human experience becomes the raw material for computational capitalism. 
* Big Tech often treats the internet — and by extension the behaviours, emotions, and knowledge embedded within it — as a new kind of Terra Nullius. As Mbembe notes, “our mind and psychic life have become the main raw material which digital capitalism aims at capturing.” 
* AI companies scrape global data streams and convert them into proprietary models, claiming ownership over what is fundamentally **collective human knowledge**. (Readings, Week 9)

**Extractivism**
* This is **Extractivism 2.0**: the appropriation of data without meaningful consent, compensation, or accountability. (Slides, Week 9) 
* The planetary ethic Mbembe proposes thus demands that we view data not as a private resource to be mined, but as a **shared heritage** requiring democratic governance and stewardship. 
* I agree with this view. If the Earth itself must be protected from enclosure, then so too must the digital commons. 
* Ethical technological development must resist extractive logics and instead cultivate transparency, reciprocity, and collective ownership of the knowledge that AI systems depend on.
